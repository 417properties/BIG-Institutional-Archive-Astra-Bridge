Yes. I would adopt this as the **Phase-1 Exhaustion Standard**.

The important improvement here is that it does **not** define exhaustion as “we ran a lot of substrates.” It defines exhaustion as a change in the economics of learning: **another Phase-1 observation is now expected to teach BIG less than working across the evidence already accumulated.**

I would organize your proof set into five gates:

1. **Field-exercise gate** — 84/84 authorized substrate positions are materially exercised **or explicitly dispositioned**, coverage is sufficiently broad, and no important executable Phase-1 cells are being ignored.
2. **Learning-saturation gate** — declining marginal yield, novelty collapse, repeated low-yield waves, convergence, and increasing replication/causal-strengthening rather than new lesson formation.
3. **Epistemic-resolution gate** — L1/L2/L3 learning is distinguishable, contradictions are bounded, and every unresolved item has a known evidence class and destination.
4. **Capacity/backlog gate** — the Phase-1 executable backlog is exhausted or dispositioned, and additional worker capacity would not materially improve learning because the constraint has moved downstream.
