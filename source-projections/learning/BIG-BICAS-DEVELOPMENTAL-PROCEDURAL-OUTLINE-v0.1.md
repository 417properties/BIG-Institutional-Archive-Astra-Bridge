# BIG Institutional Capital Accounting System (BICAS)
## Developmental Accounting Procedural Outline v0.1

**Artifact ID:** BIG-BICAS-DEVELOPMENTAL-PROCEDURAL-OUTLINE-v0.1  
**Status:** CANDIDATE / EXPERIMENTAL / NON-DOCTRINAL  
**Primary Owner for Developmental Operationalization:** BIG Development Office / Dev  
**Strategic Origin:** Strata / Founder planning discussion  
**Lifecycle Position:** Development Stage — intended to run in parallel with IEF developmental work  
**Operational Authority:** NONE  
**Financial-Statement Authority:** NONE  
**Doctrine / Canonical Promotion Authority:** NONE unless separately authorized  
**Purpose:** Create an evidence-grounded institutional-capital accounting system that measures how BIG accumulates, uses, transfers, depreciates, and compounds institutional capability across evolutionary phases.

---

# 0. CONTROL BOUNDARY

BICAS is not:

- BIG's financial balance sheet;
- GAAP or tax accounting;
- a market valuation;
- a claim that BIG has already demonstrated institutional compounding;
- a single "BIG intelligence score";
- authorization to advance any substrate from one IEF phase to another;
- authorization to change BIG doctrine, governance, or architecture;
- permission to assign speculative dollar valuations to unvalidated learning.

BICAS is:

> **a developmental management-accounting and measurement system for tracking institutional-capital formation from evidence.**

BICAS should initially operate as an instrumented research framework.  
It should earn refinement through use.

**Rule:** measurement must not create authority.

---

# 1. CORE HYPOTHESIS

BIG's Development Stage may create durable institutional assets before BIG is operational or revenue-producing.

Those assets may include:

- validated institutional knowledge;
- improved reasoning and predictive judgment;
- knowledge of substrate strengths, limits, economics, and learning behavior;
- better learning mechanisms;
- reusable contextual and causal lineage;
- improved coordination mechanisms;
- governance competence;
- frontier-recalibration capability;
- future strategic optionality.

The working hypothesis is:

> **Developmental experience can create persistent institutional capability, and persistent institutional capability can produce future learning and productive services.**

This hypothesis must be measured rather than assumed.

---

# 2. EXTERNAL-MODEL RESEARCH BASIS

BICAS is recalibrated from established external approaches to intangible and organizational capital.

External frameworks commonly distinguish:

1. **Capital Stock**  
   The durable asset or capability accumulated over time.

2. **Capital Services**  
   The productive benefit generated by that capital during a period.

3. **Capital Formation / Investment**  
   Resources expended to create durable capability.

4. **Depreciation / Obsolescence / Impairment**  
   Loss of usefulness due to time, technological change, invalidation, missing dependencies, or context loss.

5. **Multiple Valuation Lenses**  
   - creation cost;
   - replacement/reconstruction cost;
   - productive-service/income value;
   - market/comparable value where defensible;
   - option value under uncertainty.

BIG adds an unusually important capability:

> **event-level causal provenance of how institutional capital was formed.**

The objective is therefore not merely to estimate capital stock.

The objective is to preserve and measure its **genealogy**.

---

# 3. DEFINITION — BIG INSTITUTIONAL CAPITAL

**BIG Institutional Capital (BIC):**

> The persistent, evidence-supported stock of institutional capability accumulated through experience, learning, reasoning, organization, governance, context, frontier recalibration, and validated developmental change that increases BIG's future capacity to understand, learn, decide, coordinate, or produce.

For a capability to count as institutional capital, it should increasingly satisfy:

- persistence beyond one substrate/runtime;
- evidence of usefulness;
- identifiable provenance;
- valid epistemic status;
- reuse or transfer potential;
- some defensible connection to future institutional capability.

A successful substrate output alone is not institutional capital.

A remembered fact alone is not institutional capital.

A Candidate lesson alone is not validated institutional capital.

# 4. BICAS CAPITAL ACCOUNTS

BICAS v0.1 opens nine primary institutional-capital accounts.

## K — Knowledge Capital

Validated understanding of:

- frontier primitives;
- domains;
- causal relationships;
- recurring patterns;
- constraints;
- environmental realities.

**Example:** a work primitive repeatedly identified across multiple frontier surfaces.

---

## R — Reasoning Capital

Validated institutional improvement in:

- prediction;
- metacognition;
- problem framing;
- uncertainty handling;
- stopping judgment;
- value-of-information reasoning;
- reasoning allocation;
- executive judgment.

**Example:** evidence that the predictive/metacognitive reasoning loop improves later decisions.

---

## S — Substrate Capital

Institutional understanding of:

- substrate strengths and weaknesses;
- treatment response;
- context sensitivity;
- cost/performance;
- reliability;
- suitable primitive/task classes;
- failure patterns;
- learning trajectories.

**Example:** evidence that one substrate/configuration is reliably superior for a specific primitive at lower cost.

---

## L — Learning Capital

Capability that improves BIG's ability to learn from later experience.

Includes:

- learning-yield improvement;
- better experiment construction;
- better delta recognition;
- improved falsification;
- retrospective yield;
- improvement in future learning efficiency.

**Example:** an earlier delta causes later slices to produce more useful learning.

---

## C — Coordination Capital

Institutional capability for organizing multiple intelligences into coherent productive systems.

Includes:

- task decomposition;
- routing;
- dependency handling;
- worker/evaluator separation;
- escalation;
- concurrency;
- synthesis;
- whole-system coherence.

**Example:** coordinated substrate output measurably exceeds equivalent isolated substrate output.

---

## G — Governance Capital

Institutional capability for using intelligence while preserving:

- authority;
- provenance;
- Candidate/current distinction;
- One Active Truth;
- bounded permissions;
- escalation;
- consequence controls;
- safe stopping and reversal.

**Example:** a governance mechanism allows faster development without increasing authority ambiguity.

---

## X — Contextual Capital

Persistent and reusable:

- causal lineage;
- relational context;
- institutional history;
- decision-sufficient context;
- projection/reconstruction capability;
- provenance.

**Example:** a fresh substrate reconstructs why an accepted institutional change became necessary.

---

## F — Frontier-Recalibration Capital

Capability to:

- detect frontier advancement;
- understand the actual capability delta;
- determine BIG relevance;
- test a BIG-native application;
- substitute external capability for obsolete internal architecture;
- absorb frontier improvements at low cost.

**Example:** BIG detects a new external harness capability and retires unnecessary bespoke development.

---

## O — Optionality Capital

Future pathways made feasible by accumulated institutional capability.

Includes:

- provider switching;
- new domains;
- new operating modes;
- cheap bounded experiments;
- future business opportunities;
- new architecture choices;
- ability to delay irreversible commitments.

**Example:** substrate-independence allows BIG to exploit a superior future model without rebuilding institutional state.

# 5. FUNDAMENTAL CAPITAL-STOCK EQUATION

For each capital account and for the total institutional-capital system:

**Ending Institutional Capital Stock**

= Beginning Stock  
+ Validated Capital Formation  
+ Learning Reproduction / Spillovers  
+ Reactivated Historical Capital  
+ Positive Revaluation where justified  
− Depreciation / Obsolescence  
− Invalidated Capital  
− Context / Transfer Impairment  
− Retirement / Write-off

This is a management-accounting equation, not a financial-reporting equation.

# 6. CAPITAL RECOGNITION STATES

Institutional experience should move through evidence states.

1. **RAW EVENT**  
   Something occurred.

2. **PRESERVED EVIDENCE**  
   The event and relevant provenance are recorded.

3. **OBSERVATION**  
   A potentially meaningful signal is identified.

4. **CANDIDATE CAPITAL**  
   A plausible reusable institutional asset is proposed.

5. **TESTED CAPITAL**  
   The Candidate has undergone discrimination, replication, comparison, falsification, or another appropriate test.

6. **VALIDATED CAPITAL**  
   Evidence supports institutional retention as useful capability.

7. **PRODUCTIVE CAPITAL**  
   The asset demonstrably improves later learning, judgment, coordination, cost, or output.

8. **MATURE CAPITAL**  
   The asset has survived repeated cross-context use and temporal challenge.

Parallel states:

- **DORMANT** — valid but currently unused.
- **IMPAIRED** — usefulness is reduced or uncertain.
- **RETIRED** — superseded or no longer productive.
- **INVALIDATED** — evidence shows the claimed capability should no longer be treated as valid.

Retirement or invalidation does not require deletion of provenance.

Historical state remains evidence.

# 8. THREE LEVELS OF LEARNING MUST BE SEPARATED

BICAS must not collapse all learning into one category.

## L1 — Substrate Learning

Did the finite substrate change its behavior based on prior experience?

Examples:

- changed prediction;
- changed reasoning allocation;
- changed retrieval strategy;
- changed tool use;
- changed framing;
- reduced repeated error.

---

## L2 — Institutional Learning

Did BIG learn something from the substrate's experience that persisted beyond the substrate?

Examples:

- changed institutional understanding;
- changed substrate selection;
- changed Suit/Glasses design;
- changed primitive classification;
- changed experimental protocol;
- changed R&D interpretation.

---

## L3 — Learning-System Improvement

Did institutional learning improve the developmental environment such that other substrates subsequently learned or performed better?

This is the principal compounding discriminator.

Conceptually:

**substrate experience  
→ institutional delta  
→ environment/process refinement  
→ changed future substrate behavior  
→ greater later learning yield**

BICAS should explicitly trace this lineage where evidence exists.

# 9. CORE CAPITAL-SERVICE METRICS

BICAS should continuously measure, where feasible:

## Reuse Rate

How often is prior institutional learning used in later work?

## Transfer Breadth

Does the asset transfer across:

- one task;
- one primitive;
- one frontier surface;
- multiple surfaces;
- enterprise-wide contexts?

## Persistence Rate

How much useful capability survives substrate/runtime replacement?

## Learning Reproduction Rate (LRR)

How many validated downstream learning units are causally supported as descendants of prior learning?

## Learning Yield per Dollar

Validated developmental learning divided by total developmental resource cost.

## Productivity Lift

Difference between performance with and without the institutional asset under comparable conditions.

## Prediction Lift

Change in forecast/calibration quality attributable to accumulated institutional learning.

## Reasoning Efficiency Lift

Equivalent or improved decision quality with lower reasoning cost.

## Correction Burden Reduction

Change in Founder/CEA/Dev/evaluator correction required.

## Reconstruction Burden Reduction

Change in effort required to restore office/task competence after substrate replacement.

## Frontier Absorption Efficiency

Useful BIG capability received from external frontier advancement relative to BIG's cost to discover/test/recalibrate it.

## Coordination Surplus

Observed coordinated output minus expected output from equivalent isolated substrates.

## Option Expansion

New feasible actions or future pathways made possible by current institutional capability.

## Capital Utilization

Fraction of validated capital stock that is currently producing measurable services.

## Capital Depreciation Rate

Loss of useful capability due to technological change, invalidation, dependency loss, or obsolescence.

# 10. FRONTIER RECALIBRATION RETURN (FRR)

BICAS should experimentally track:

## FRR

**Benefit obtained by BIG from an external frontier advance**

divided by

**BIG's cost to detect, understand, test, recalibrate, integrate, and govern that advance**

Benefit may initially be measured as:

- avoided internal development;
- lower substrate expense;
- lower tool expense;
- productivity increase;
- new capability;
- faster learning;
- better reliability;
- reduced architecture;
- expanded optionality.

FRR is not required to be monetary during early Development.

# 11. CAPITAL CONFIDENCE GRADES

All recognized capital should carry an evidence grade.

### C0 — RECORDED

Event exists.

### C1 — OBSERVED

Plausible learning/capability identified.

### C2 — REPLICATED

Pattern repeated under materially relevant conditions.

### C3 — TRANSFERRED

Capability usefully transfers beyond the original local case.

### C4 — CAUSALLY SUPPORTED

Controlled evidence materially supports attribution to the institutional delta.

### C5 — PRODUCTIVELY DEMONSTRATED

The capital provides measurable institutional service.

### C6 — ECONOMICALLY DEMONSTRATED

The asset produces defensible measurable economic benefit.

**Rule:** low-confidence capital may be tracked but must not be represented as mature economic value.

# 12. PHASE-1 BICAS PROCEDURE

Phase 1 is the first large-scale capital-observation field.

Primary purpose:

> determine whether primitive developmental experience produces persistent and reusable institutional capital.

Expected dominant capital accounts:

- K — Knowledge;
- R — Reasoning;
- S — Substrate;
- L — Learning;
- X — Context;
- F — Frontier Recalibration.

## Phase-1 Operating Loop

For each recursive slice:

1. substrate receives task / primitive exposure;
2. substrate forms prediction or expected state where applicable;
3. work is performed;
4. outcome is captured;
5. prediction/outcome delta is recorded;
6. watcher/observer records behavior and material signals;
7. recorder preserves evidence/provenance;
8. evaluator/R&D distinguishes noise from potential learning;
9. Candidate ICER is generated where justified;
10. Candidate capital is tested or accumulated for later cross-comparison;
11. validated delta is promoted only through authorized institutional process;
12. later slices record whether prior learning changed subsequent behavior;
13. L1/L2/L3 learning is separated;
14. depreciation/obsolescence risk remains active.

- K — Knowledge;

The 20 records remain provenance.

The consolidated principle becomes the higher-level active capital object.

# 19. SHADOW VALUATION LEDGER

BICAS should maintain a separate strategic valuation ledger.

This ledger is:

- non-GAAP;
- non-tax;
- non-market-value;
- non-doctrinal;
- decision-support only.

For sufficiently developed institutional assets, evaluate four lenses.

## A. Creation Cost

What did BIG spend to create the capability?

## B. Replacement / Reconstruction Cost

What would it cost today to recreate equivalent capability after total loss?

Include:

- compute;
- model/API;
- tools;
- experiments;
- Dev/CEA/Professor effort;
- Founder reconstruction burden;
- elapsed time;
- failure repetition;
- opportunity cost where defensible.

## C. Productive-Service / Income Value

What measurable future cost savings, productive output, risk reduction, or economic benefit does the capital produce?

Use only when evidence supports it.

## D. Option Value

What future paths are feasible because the institutional asset exists?

Especially useful during pre-operational Development when immediate cash flow is absent.

No single lens controls automatically.

# 20. CAPITAL DEPRECIATION / WRITE-DOWN PROCEDURE

R&D should actively search for institutional-capital impairment.

Triggers may include:

- new frontier model reduces need for prior scaffolding;
- provider productizes a capability BIG was building;
- prior lesson no longer transfers;
- better experiment falsifies an earlier belief;
- context projection becomes unusable;
- dependency disappears;
- substrate economics materially change;
- prior architecture becomes unnecessary;
- newer evidence reduces confidence.

Possible dispositions:

- retain;
- revalue;
- impair;
- consolidate;
- reactivate;
- retire;
- invalidate;
- investigate.

A capital write-down may represent **positive learning yield** if it prevents future sunk cost.
