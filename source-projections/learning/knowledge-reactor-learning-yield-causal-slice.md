## Reactor origin

Historical reconstruction records that the **Knowledge Reactor came from the Founder’s compounding-learning idea**: incubation was compared to an atomic chain reaction in which an outcome branches into success/failure, failure generates “why” and “how to correct,” and those produce additional learning units. The Professor refined the metaphor into a **controlled knowledge reactor** to add governance, containment, quality control, safe-stop mechanisms, and useful output.

The resulting cycle was recorded as:

**Capture → Decompose → Attribute → Challenge → Validate → Refine → Propagate → Monitor.**

The Reactor was explicitly distinguished from Brain/memory and Cortex/reasoning: its role was to convert experience/evidence into validated learning, patterns, refinements, and momentum.

## Learning Yield correction

The Founder explicitly rejected treating unsuccessful paths primarily as “failures.” Course Zero formalized:

- Learning Yield Opportunity;
- Wrong-Path Recognition Point;
- Diagnostic Branch;
- Recovery Pivot;
- Negative Knowledge;
- Transfer Test.

Rejected branches retain conditional negative knowledge rather than being erased.

## Later control problem

The Professor review later proposed **marginal learning yield** as a possible regulator of recursive inquiry rather than arbitrary “N levels” stopping. A mature BIG may become better at identifying which questions are worth asking.

BICAS later treats stopping judgment and learning efficiency as candidate Reasoning/Learning Capital, while BSIC D7 adds learning-assimilation capacity and separates record volume, work throughput, learning throughput, and compound-learning throughput.

These later controls do not prove an optimal learning velocity; they establish the need to measure whether learning intake can be assimilated without creating option explosion, stale queues, or low-yield recursive inquiry.
